## [Terry Tao: AI Will Increase, Not Reduce, Demand for Mathematicians](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/24/were-gonna-need-a-lot-more-mathematicians/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Terence Tao published a blog essay titled "We're gonna need a lot more mathematicians," arguing that AI tools will expand rather than shrink the need for human mathematical expertise. The post triggered a large Hacker News discussion (352 points, 456 comments) about comprehension, LLM-generated code, and the purpose of mathematical training. Tao is one of the most influential living mathematicians, so his argument cuts against the common assumption that LLMs will simply automate mathematical and programming work. The debate matters for educators, researchers, and engineers deciding how much formal training and domain understanding humans still need. The essay emphasizes that human comprehension and verification remain the bottleneck: an AI output is only useful if a person can understand why it works and justify confidence in its correctness. Commenters extend this to software, noting that LLM-generated solutions often suffer from classic XY problems, poor user experience, and unnecessary complexity that accrues future maintenance debt.

hackernews · srcreigh · Sep 26, 02:46 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49852717)

**Background**: Terence Tao is a Fields Medal-winning mathematician at UCLA known for work spanning number theory, harmonic analysis, and combinatorics, and he has written extensively about how AI is changing mathematical practice. Large language models such as Claude, ChatGPT, and similar tools can now generate code and plausible mathematical arguments, prompting debate over whether traditional mathematical and computer-science training is still worthwhile. The Hacker News thread reflects a broader industry conversation about "vibe coding" and how much engineers should rely on AI-generated code.

**Discussion**: Commenters largely agreed with Tao that comprehension cannot be outsourced, with one noting that they used to scrutinize every line of Claude's code and caught problems in nearly every response but now catch fewer, raising the question of whether the model improved or their own vigilance eroded under shipping pressure. Another argued that studying math and computer science is meant to transform the mind rather than produce commodities, so even superintelligence is useless without humans capable of comprehending it. Others observed that colleagues delegating wholesale to Claude run into XY problems, poor UX, and over-complex solutions, reinforcing the need for domain understanding, while one parent described the joy of vibe coding a game with their ten-year-old as a personal counterpoint to fears about AI.
