## [Terence Tao's 'Math 2.0' Slides Urge AI to Be a Good Citizen in Mathematics](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Terence Tao released the slides for his Caltech 2026 talk 'Math 2.0', posted to his blog on 10 October 2026, in which he lays out the historical 'Math 1.0' state of mathematics and the current AI-induced disruption of his own field. His central argument is that AI systems should behave as good citizens of mathematics by extending and maintaining the discipline's shared 'codebase' of theories, definitions and proofs, rather than merely producing solutions. The slides sparked a large Hacker News debate with 351 comments about AI, understanding, and applied science. Tao is one of the most influential living mathematicians, so his framing of AI as a participant in mathematical practice rather than a mere tool carries weight in how the field navigates recent AI results in theorem proving. The talk speaks directly to a growing anxiety among researchers that AI could rapidly solve long-standing open problems, forcing mathematicians to rethink grant proposals, research directions, and the social structure of the discipline. Tao's metaphor that 'open problems are lighthouses not destinations' reframes unsolved problems as signals pointing to where the theoretical codebase needs extension, and he argues current math AI is not a 'good citizen' because it does not strive for the most elegant, well-integrated additions. Commenters also noted that the talk was significantly rewritten from its original abstract due to recent events, and that it sits atop the formal-math infrastructure of proof assistants such as Lean and its community library mathlib.

hackernews · Anon84 · Oct 11, 13:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50043026)

**Background**: Tao describes mathematics as having been in a largely stable 'Math 1.0' state for almost a century, a period in which results were produced by human mathematicians reading, refining and building on one another's work. Recent AI systems have begun proving or contributing to genuine mathematical results, and automated theorem proving is the subfield of automated reasoning that deals with proving theorems by computer program. A parallel development is the rise of formal proof assistants such as Lean and its community-driven library mathlib, which encode definitions and proofs in a machine-checkable form — the closest real-world analogue to Tao's 'codebase' of mathematics.

## [Japan Declares Cyber Emergency After Attacks on 20+ Major Firms](https://www.ft.com/content/2d4333d1-8119-44a4-a5a5-2ee9ef2544fa?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Japanese government has declared a "cyberspace emergency" after more than 20 major companies were hit by cyberattacks in recent weeks, affecting railways, automakers, hospitals and government facilities and potentially exposing tens of millions of customer records. In response, the Financial Services Agency ordered banks to stop accepting driver's licenses as identity proof for opening new accounts and to require chip-embedded identification documents instead. This is a rare nationally declared cybersecurity emergency for one of the world's largest economies, signaling that attacks have reached critical infrastructure and consumer finance at scale. The forced change to identity-verification rules will immediately affect how millions of Japanese residents open bank accounts, and the incident is likely to accelerate government spending and regulation on cyber defense. JR East said its ticket booking system may have leaked the email addresses of 1.67 million customers, and several of the incidents have been linked to a ransomware attack on IDC Frontier, a data center subsidiary of SoftBank. Experts describe Japan's defenses as weak and note that AI is lowering the barrier to launching complex attacks, though there is currently no evidence tying the latest wave of attacks to AI.

**Background**: Ransomware is malware that encrypts a victim's systems and data and demands payment for their release, and it is frequently used against hospitals, transport operators and other organizations that cannot afford downtime. In Japan, the My Number Card is the government-issued chip-based identity document that authorities now want used instead of driver's licenses for financial account verification, because chip cards are harder to forge than paper-based documents. IDC Frontier is a SoftBank-affiliated data center and cloud provider, meaning an intrusion there can cascade into many downstream corporate customers at once.
